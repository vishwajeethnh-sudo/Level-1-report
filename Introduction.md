# Mechanical Tasks
## Introduction To Designing 
Mechanical design is an essential branch of mechanical engineering that involves the design, development, analysis, and improvement of mechanical components, devices, equipment, and manufacturing systems. It combines engineering principles with technical knowledge to develop products that meet specific functional requirements while maintaining accuracy, reliability, and efficiency. Mechanical design also involves selecting suitable materials, determining dimensions, and ensuring that components can be manufactured and assembled effectively.
### *Fundamentals of Design*
#### 1. Drawing, Drafting and Design

Drawing is a basic representation of an object that provides an initial understanding of its shape and features. 

Drafting is the process of preparing accurate engineering drawings using standard dimensions, scales, measurements, and conventions. 

Design involves developing a plan or specification for a product or system, considering its function, geometry, and performance requirements.

#### 2. CAD, CAM and CAE

Computer-Aided Design (CAD) is used to create, modify, and optimise two-dimensional drawings and three-dimensional models using computer software. It includes sketching, part modelling, sheet metal design, surface modelling, and drafting.

Computer-Aided Manufacturing (CAM) uses software to control machine tools, support machining operations, and generate numerical control (NC) programs for manufacturing components.

Computer-Aided Engineering (CAE) involves using computer software to perform engineering analysis, simulation, validation, and optimisation. It includes techniques such as Finite Element Analysis (FEA), Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD), and Multibody Dynamics (MBD).

#### 3. Engineering Drawing and Projection Methods

Engineering drawing communicates the geometry and dimensions of mechanical components through standardised representations. Orthographic projection represents an object using separate views, such as the front, top, and side views, whereas isometric projection represents its three-dimensional shape in a single view.

The main orthographic projection methods are first-angle and third-angle projection.

#### 4. Fits, Clearances, Tolerances and Allowances

Fits describe the relationship between two mating components, such as a shaft and a hole. The three main types are clearance fit, interference fit, and transition fit. A clearance fit provides space between mating parts, an interference fit creates a tight connection, and a transition fit may result in either a small clearance or a small interference.

Tolerance is the permissible variation in a component's specified dimension. It may be expressed using limit, unilateral, or bilateral tolerances. 

Allowance refers to the intentional difference between the dimensions of mating components at their maximum-material conditions. These concepts are essential for ensuring proper assembly, functionality, and interchangeability.

#### 5. Geometric Dimensioning and Tolerancing (GD&T)

Geometric Dimensioning and Tolerancing (GD&T) is a standardised engineering language used to communicate the dimensional and geometric requirements of components. It specifies acceptable variations in the form, orientation, location, profile, and runout of features.

GD&T uses symbols and feature control frames to define geometric requirements. Datums act as reference points, axes, or planes for manufacturing and inspection. Important geometric controls include straightness, flatness, circularity, cylindricity, parallelism, perpendicularity, angularity, position, concentricity, symmetry, and runout.
